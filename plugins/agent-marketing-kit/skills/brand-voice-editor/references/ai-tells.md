# Generic AI tells to remove

These patterns make real estate content read as machine-written. Remove or rewrite them unless the agent's Brand Brain says they genuinely talk this way.

## Openers and transitions
- "Let's dive in," "Let's talk," "Here's the thing," "Here's where it gets nuanced/interesting"
- "In today's market," "In today's fast-paced world," "Now more than ever"
- "It's time to retire that myth," "If you've heard X, think again"
- "So what's actually happening?" used as a heading or transition when a plain heading would do
- "The bottom line:", "At the end of the day," "When all is said and done"

## Sentence shapes
- "Not X. Not Y. Z." triple negation build-ups ("Not what it was worth when you bought it. Not what your neighbor guessed.")
- "It's not just X, it's Y" and "X isn't a nice-to-have anymore, it's the difference between…"
- Colon or dash reveals: "That last stat is the big one." followed by the explanation
- Rhetorical question immediately answered by the writer, more than once per post
- Lists of three adjectives or three clauses in a row, repeatedly
- Heavy em dash use (often banned outright in the Brand Brain)

## Words and phrases
- navigate, landscape, journey, leverage, unlock, elevate, seamless, robust, game-changer, ever-evolving, delve, realm, tapestry, testament
- "knowledge is power," "put your best foot forward," "inside and out," "sitting on more equity than you realize" (fine once if the agent says it; cut if it's filler)
- "smooth, confident sale," "peace of mind," "no guesswork required" as stock closers
- Real estate clichés: stunning, dream home, nestled, must-see, hidden gem, luxury living, turnkey (unless literally true and the agent uses it)

## Structure
- Every section ending on a tidy one-line moral
- Generic CTA ("give us a call!", "reach out today") when the agent has a specific standard CTA
- Exclamation points the agent wouldn't use
- Headings that are questions in every section

## What to put in their place
- The agent's own phrases from "Words I Use"
- One concrete local detail (a neighborhood name, a street, a season, a local event) that the Brand Brain or the draft supports
- A real proof theme from Section 6
- Plain statements of fact followed by what it means for this reader, in the agent's rhythm
- A `[ADD: …]` placeholder where only the agent can supply the specific story
